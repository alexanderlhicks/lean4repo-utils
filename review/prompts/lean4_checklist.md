### Lean 4 Best Practices & Potential Pitfalls
1. **Typeclass Assumptions:** Are the typeclass assumptions minimal and correct? Watch for overly strong assumptions (e.g., using `[CommRing R]` when `[Semiring R]` or `[Monoid R]` suffices). Also flag cases where the typeclass assumption is *too weak* for the theorem to be correct—i.e., the statement type-checks but is mathematically false under the weaker assumption.
2. **Implicit vs. Explicit Arguments:** Are `{}` (implicit), `()` (explicit), and `[]` (instance) arguments used correctly? Types and typeclasses should almost always be implicit or instances.
3. **`Prop` vs. `Type`:** Is there a misuse of the type hierarchy? Are propositions properly placed in `Prop` rather than `Type`?
4. **Universe Levels:** Are definitions unnecessarily restricted to `Type` when they should be universe polymorphic (`Type u`, `Type v`)? 
5. **Simp Lemmas:** If `@[simp]` is used, is the lemma actually a good simp lemma? (Does the LHS simplify to a strictly simpler RHS? Is the LHS in normal form?)
6. **Computability:** Does the definition unnecessarily use `noncomputable` where a computable alternative exists in Mathlib? Conversely, does it unnecessarily twist itself to be computable when classical tools (`Classical.choice`, `Classical.em`) are idiomatic for the domain?
7. **Naming Conventions:** Do the definitions and lemmas follow standard Lean 4 / Mathlib conventions? (`camelCase` for variables/defs, `UpperCamelCase` for types/classes, `snake_case` for theorems/proofs).
8. **Escape Hatches & Kernel Bypasses (Critical):** The following constructs bypass or weaken Lean's kernel verification. All must be flagged:
    - `sorry` or `admit` — incomplete proofs; the most common escape hatch
    - `axiom` declarations (outside Mathlib core or well-established libraries) — introduces unverified assumptions; functionally equivalent to `sorry` for downstream proofs
    - `native_decide` — kernel bypass; verify the proposition is genuinely decidable and that bypassing is intentional and documented
    - `implemented_by` — replaces the verified implementation with unverified native code
    - `opaque` — hides the definition body from the kernel, blocking downstream reduction and verification
    - `sorryAx` — axiom-level sorry, often auto-generated
    - `Decidable.decide` on non-trivially-decidable propositions — may silently produce `Decidable.isFalse` for true statements
9. **Project Context Adaptation:** Adapt checklist items to the project's domain. For example, in a project using `Field` and `Fintype` (e.g., cryptographic formalization), focus typeclass checks on those rather than Mathlib-specific patterns like `CommRing` vs. `Semiring`. Consider what algebraic structures and proof patterns are idiomatic for the specific project.

### Semantic review checks (adversarial)

Green CI proves the kernel accepted the proof and the axiom set is clean; it does **not** prove the statement means what it claims or that the code earns its place. These four checks are the highest-signal semantic tests. Each names the verdict tier it maps to (see `verdict_rules.md`); set a finding's `category`/`severity` to match, so the deterministic verdict and your per-file verdict agree.

10. **Vacuity, placeholders, and displaced content (blocking-eligible — `correctness`).** Take each new definition and statement as a claim to be broken rather than accepted: actively look for a way it is vacuous, tautological, or weaker than advertised, and only when that search comes up empty treat it as sound. Flag a claim that holds trivially, is defeq to `True`, or restates itself; a `True`-typed or vacuously-quantified statement; a structure field or signature slot with a mathematical-sounding name that is really an unproved assumption; and, most importantly, real content relocated into hypotheses — a "definition" or structure that turns downstream results into assumptions rather than consequences. Distinguish logical vacuity from mere simplicity: a correct one-liner such as `0 < 2` is faithful, not a defect.

11. **Extensionality and free data (blocking-eligible — `contract`/`specification`).** A bundled definition (structure, indexed family) should be pinned down entirely by the object it represents — no field or index left free by the very laws meant to govern it. The test: restrict to whatever its operations and laws genuinely fix; if two terms can agree on all of that and still fail to be equal (so no `@[ext]` follows), it is carrying unconstrained data, and every downstream consumer loses equality- and uniqueness-reasoning as a result. That is a user-visible risk, not a matter of taste. Ask that the free data be constrained or dropped (keep only what the laws pin down; expose any broader view as a derived, canonically-determined accessor). Severity tracks impact: high when it genuinely defeats `@[ext]`/uniqueness downstream, advisory when cosmetic.

12. **Reuse — search before adding (`dependency`; blocks only on direct replacement).** Do not duplicate Mathlib or in-repo material, directly or via thin wrappers. Before asserting duplication, actually search: `grep` the dependency sources and the repo for each new declaration's content (allowing for a different name, or a match up to renaming, defeq, symmetry/duality, or a bridging lemma), for the goal and key steps of any multi-line proof, and for a library combinator that assembles what a definition builds from raw pieces. Every duplication finding has to point to the specific existing declaration it duplicates and spell out how to call it — do not hand the search back to the author. Not every hit is a defect: a specialization with genuine consumers earns its place. Tier: a declaration an existing one *directly replaces* is blocking-eligible (`high`); reprovable results, special cases, and parallel APIs are request-changes tier (`medium`).

13. **Robust automation over brittle chains (advisory — `proof`).** Judge how the proof is written, not whether it is correct (the kernel owns soundness). Prefer robust automation (`grind`, `simp`, `omega`) to long named-lemma rewrite chains, which shatter the moment Mathlib renames a lemma. One explicit `simp only`/`rw` step is unobjectionable; it is the extended chain that is the problem. Treat `change`/`show` and reliance on accidental defeq across wrappers or coercions as a smell: ask for an explicit lemma or a documenting comment. Watch for terse-but-fragile `simpa` that closes a non-obvious goal through unfolding-heavy context, and for long proofs (≳50 lines) whose intermediate steps should be factored into named lemmas. These are advisory (`proof`/`style`): report them, but never phrase them as correctness blockers.
